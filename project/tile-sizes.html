A simple guide

Why Does Tile Size Matter?

The right tile size can help create the look you want while making installation and maintenance easier. Smaller tiles have more grout lines, while larger tiles usually create a more continuous appearance. Your room dimensions, floor condition, tile material, and personal style all matter.

Common Floor Tile Sizes

These are common dimensions to consider when planning a residential flooring project.

Small format

30 × 30 cm

A compact square tile that can work well in smaller areas and spaces where more grout lines or a detailed pattern are desired.

Common uses

  • Small rooms and utility areas
  • Compact balconies
  • Decorative floor layouts

Consider: More grout lines may mean more cleaning and maintenance.

Medium format

40 × 40 cm

A versatile square tile that offers a balance between a compact tile and a larger modern format.

Common uses

  • Bedrooms
  • Smaller living areas
  • Hallways and everyday spaces

Consider: Check the room layout so you can reduce awkward cuts around the edges.

Large square format

60 × 60 cm

A popular square format that gives floors a clean, balanced appearance and works in many modern interiors.

Common uses

  • Living rooms
  • Bedrooms and dining areas
  • Open-plan interiors

Consider: Larger tiles need a suitably flat surface and careful installation.

Large rectangular format

60 × 120 cm

A long rectangular tile that can create an elegant, spacious appearance with fewer grout lines than smaller tiles.

Common uses

  • Large living rooms
  • Open-plan floors
  • Contemporary interiors

Consider: Plan handling, transportation, cutting, and installation before choosing this format.

Before you decide

How Do You Choose the Right Size?

Start by measuring your room, then consider the appearance you want, the condition of the floor, and your budget. A larger tile is not automatically the best choice for every room.

  • ✓ Measure the room. Know its length, width, and total floor area.
  • ✓ Think about the layout. Consider edges, corners, doorways, and cuts.
  • ✓ Check the tile specifications. Confirm the actual dimensions and recommended installation method.
  • ✓ Allow for waste. Extra tiles may be needed for cuts, breakage, and future repairs.
